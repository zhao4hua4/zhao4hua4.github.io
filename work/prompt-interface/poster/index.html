01 / The interface

A prompt.
Six layers of choice.

Natural language inference asks whether one sentence follows from another, contradicts it, or leaves it undecided. The logical task stays fixed. Its interface has several moving parts.

Take the prompt apart.

Switch the answer format to see which layers change. The later experiments compare Chinese and English versions of these interfaces.

Semantic labels

Category names are visible and serve as the output: entailment, neutral or contradiction.

Illustrative English prompt assembled for this page. The sentences below are authored examples, not XNLI items.

  1. 01 Input

    Sentence 1A small boat crosses the lake.

    Sentence 2A boat is moving.

  2. 02 Directive
    Classify the relationship between Sentence 1 and Sentence 2.
  3. 03 Definitions

    Sentence 2 must be true if Sentence 1 is true.

    Sentence 2 may be true or false if Sentence 1 is true.

    Sentence 2 must be false if Sentence 1 is true.

  4. 04 Category names
    entailment · neutral · contradiction
  5. 05 Symbol binding
    Each relation is answered with its category name.
  6. 06 Output contract
    Return only: entailment, neutral or contradiction.
Compare the three experimental interfaces

Semantic labels: category names and relation definitions are visible; the response is a category name. ABC only: category names are removed, the definitions remain, and the response is A, B or C. Anchored ABC: category names return inside those options, while the output remains A, B or C.

Removing labels changes both their visibility and the output form. Anchored ABC helps separate their contribution by restoring names without returning to semantic-label output. These are behavioural contrasts; they do not directly identify an internal model mechanism.

Source: paper §3–4 and Appendix G.

Read the interaction

The Chinese-procedure advantage is +3.94 pp with English definitions and +1.17 pp with Chinese definitions. The procedure effect depends on definition language, giving a negative interaction (reported estimate −2.78 pp).

This behavioural localisation is specific to Llama-3.1-70B, Chinese inputs and the ABC-only interface. It does not establish that directive language dominates in every model or task.

The takeaway

Report the interface.
Understand the score.

Labels, definitions, symbols and output rules belong in the evaluation protocol. Changing them can change both the size and the direction of a multilingual contrast.

01

Separate the layers.

Record input, directive and definition language, category wording, symbol bindings and output form.

02

Test the mappings.

Average across answer assignments and report their variability alongside accuracy.

03

Localise the effect.

Use controlled changes to distinguish a broad interface contrast from the parts that contribute to it.

Study design and uncertainty

XNLI test examples: 600 per language, balanced at 200 per class, in Chinese and English. Within each input language, the same examples and gold labels are used across conditions. Decoding is deterministic (temperature 0). Five models enter the semantic-label baseline; intensive follow-ups focus on Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-70B.

ABC-only and anchored ABC results average six fixed bijective symbol mappings. Paired bootstrap intervals use 2,000 resamples of examples; they describe uncertainty in the contrast. The range and standard deviation across mappings describe a different quantity: sensitivity to answer assignment. The paper also reports paired McNemar tests.

All gains on this page are percentage points; all accuracies are percentages. Values and intervals reproduce the paper’s rounded estimates.

In three selected Chinese-interface conditions, restoring semantic-label output after anchored ABC adds 1.2–1.3 pp in accuracy. These intervals include or touch zero. This is a condition-level control, not a full Chinese-versus-English interface contrast, and it is kept separate from the gain ladder.

Scope and robustness

The evidence covers two languages, one NLI benchmark and a controlled prompt family. The paper’s term “semantic label anchoring” describes the observed behaviour; it does not establish a new internal model mechanism.

A disjoint, balanced sample of 600 examples per language checks the semantic-label baseline for Qwen3-8B and Llama-3.1-8B. Differences in gains between samples have confidence intervals including zero. This supports a bounded robustness check, not a general scaling claim.

Category-name removal also changes output form. Anchored ABC, alternate names, crossed label language, and the targeted factorial each probe a different part of that interface dependence. Source: discussion, limitations, Appendices C–G.
