Hidden in Plain Text

Make a model’s evidence interpretable.

A surface–neural audit of the cues models use to predict reader-perceived social comparison.

NLPCC 2026 Accepted

First and corresponding author

The contribution

Within reader-grounded NLP, we introduce a surface–neural cue-visibility audit for reader-perceived labels. It aligns independently derived corpus patterns with test-time model attribution under a shared reference contrast. The framework distinguishes shared, surface-only, neural-only and weak or contextual evidence.

Why it matters

An evaluation score captures predictive performance. A cue-visibility audit adds a view of the evidence behind it: which linguistic patterns a model shares with an interpretable corpus description, and where its decisions depend on platform style or discourse markers.

What the evidence shows

Across all three encoders, downward comparison has greater surface–neural overlap than upward comparison. A shared trouble-telling profile centres on pronouns, negation, reported speech, affectedness and constrained agency. Upward comparison is distributed across achievement, display and platform style. Cue-family ranks correlate at ρ = .942–.983 across encoders.

How it works

01

Build the corpus view

Discover surface cues on TRAIN using weighted log odds, keyness, dispersion, function/discourse profiles and concordance. Use NEUTRAL as a substantive reference category.

02

Build the model view

Compute contrastive attribution on 2,779 analysable TEST posts using fine-tuned Chinese RoBERTa, MacBERT and BERT. Align attributions to surface units and cue families.

03

Audit convergence and divergence

Compare top-ranked cues, vary selection cutoffs, examine prediction strata, and use masking and layer-wise cue-family checks to characterise robustness.

Research context & materials

One reader-perceived Chinese social-media corpus and three 12-layer BERT-family encoders trained with seed 42. Neural analysis uses 2,779 valid TEST rows; surface replication uses the full 2,783-row TEST split. Attribution and masking describe model behaviour, with effects varying by encoder and control.
