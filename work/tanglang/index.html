The research

My PhD project, A Grammar of Tanglang: Contact-Induced Phonological Change in an Endangered Ngwi Language, brings grammatical description together with the study of language contact. I investigate how Tanglang is organised and how its sound patterns vary across speakers and generations. The project connects these patterns with the social settings in which the language is used, placing phonological change within a broader account of grammar and everyday speech.

Why it matters

A useful linguistic evidence base connects recordings, transcription, annotation and analysis. This work develops the descriptions and contextual information that support language research, community use and future language technology, particularly where existing resources are limited.

Current research

The funded doctoral programme began in September 2026 at Trinity College Dublin, supported by a Government of Ireland Postgraduate Scholarship. It centres on two connected questions: how to describe Tanglang’s grammatical system, and how language contact shapes phonological variation across generations.

Research plan

01

Document language in use

The documentation plan combines speech recordings with speaker and situational context. Transcription, translation and annotation will keep examples connected to the circumstances in which they were produced.

02

Trace sound patterns across generations

Phonological analysis will examine how contact-related patterns vary across speakers and generations, alongside grammatical description. This connects evidence about sound change with the language’s wider structure.

03

Build a record for continued use

The intended outputs are a grammar and archived documentation with linked recordings and annotations. Community collaboration and agreed access conditions will shape materials for local use and future research.

Research context & materials

Tanglang, an endangered Ngwi language in Yunnan, China. Doctoral research in progress.

Recording and annotation materials are planned as part of the documentation programme. Public access will follow agreements with community collaborators.
