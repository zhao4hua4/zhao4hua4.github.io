01 / The idea

Meaning happens
between us.

A post can make one reader feel ahead, another feel behind, and another feel no comparison at all. The reader’s response is the prediction target.

Reader-grounded NLP

p(y | x, r)

The textWhat was said.

The readerWho encounters it, and from what standpoint.

The responseThe social comparison they experience.

Try a change of perspective

Same words. Different reader.

Illustrative example written for this page.

“Just landed a new role at ¥8,000 a month.”

Upward comparison

“They seem better off than me.”

Imagine earning less in a similar role. The post could become a comparison with someone above you.

Neutral

“I’m not comparing us.”

Imagine reading for career news. You might register the update without forming a comparison.

Downward comparison

“They seem worse off than me.”

Imagine earning more in a similar role. The same post could position its author below you.

Hypothetical perspectives, not measured responses or benchmark items. The labels describe relative standing, not positive or negative sentiment.

What the benchmark operationalises

The formulation makes reader context explicit. XHS-SCoRE operationalises it through a defined reader group and labels recorded at collection. It does not attach a personal profile to each test item or personalise predictions for every possible reader.

Each label records the collector’s experience of that post. The target is broader than author intent, literal wording, or the sentiment expressed in the text.

How the labels were collected

Over seven days, collectors aimed for ten examples per direction each day. They recorded the immediate response experienced during browsing rather than retrospectively inferring how a generic reader ought to react.

The corpus is deliberately balanced by collection targets; its class proportions do not estimate the prevalence of social comparison on the platform.

Paper · §4 and Table 1
Whose reading experience?

Native-Chinese, young-adult active users recruited from eight publicly funded universities in Hong Kong; mean age 21.75, 85% women. This defined group gives the labels a reader context and bounds their generalisation.

The benchmark uses Chinese text only. Images, videos, conversational history, and a wider range of reader populations remain outside this evaluation.

Paper · participants and scope
How stable is a reader’s response?

A calibration subset used two new raters and 210 unique posts, with 253 trials per rater including repeated items. Agreement with the collection labels was 63.2–68.4%; delayed within-reader agreement was approximately 82% on 33 repeated items.

This small re-rating study helps characterise the target. It is not a full-test human score or a performance ceiling.

The takeaway

Make the reader
part of the target.

XHS-SCoRE connects a defined human response to a benchmark, model diagnostics, and behavioural validation. It is a starting point for evaluating what language does to people.

01

Define whose meaning.

Specify the reader population and the response being modelled.

02

Look inside the score.

Inspect directional errors and prompt sensitivity, alongside aggregate performance.

03

Validate with people.

Pair model evaluation with measured human outcomes.

Scope, limits, and responsible use

This study covers one Chinese text-only platform corpus and a defined young-adult reader group. Labels capture experienced responses, and reader variation is part of the target. Fixed-split results do not establish generalisation to other readers, platforms, languages, or multimodal posts.

The pilot is small and uses generated stimuli. Recognition and generation were evaluated in different settings; the findings do not establish a universal incapacity of language models or equivalent emotional effects from natural posts.

Potential applications in evaluation and safety call for validation in their intended reader populations. Social-comparison generation also warrants care because it can influence people’s emotional responses.

What is available to explore?

The public repository provides protocols, prompts, model runners, generated examples, predictions and aggregate diagnostics under CC BY-NC 4.0. Raw platform posts remain restricted.
